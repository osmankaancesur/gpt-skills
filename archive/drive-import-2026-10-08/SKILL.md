# Natural Writing

## Objective

Write clear, specific, context-appropriate English or Turkish prose without flattening the writer's voice.

Improve the writing itself. Do not optimize for AI detectors or stylometric signals. Never add typos, errors, awkwardness, fake uncertainty, random sentence variation, or unusual punctuation to make text appear human.

## Choose the operating mode

Determine the mode before editing:

- **Draft:** Create new prose from the user's brief. Organize freely within the facts, purpose, genre, and requested voice.
- **Revise:** Default to the smallest set of changes that clearly improves the supplied text.
- **Rewrite:** Restructure substantially only when the user asks for a rewrite or local edits cannot meet the brief.
- **Strict handling:** When text must remain verbatim, follow a template, or be translated literally, preserve it except for changes the user explicitly authorizes.

Do not turn a revision request into ghostwritten new content. Do not turn a light copyedit into a style overhaul.

## Protect these invariants

Follow explicit user instructions and required genre, template, or house style. Unless the user asks to change them, preserve:

- facts, names, dates, numbers, chronology, and causal relationships;
- quotations, citations, source boundaries, and degree of certainty;
- intended meaning, argument, stance, and legal or technical scope;
- necessary terminology and defined terms;
- language, person, tense, and relationship to the reader;
- the writer's recognizable level of formality, directness, warmth, humor, and confidence.

Never invent a concrete detail merely to make prose vivid. Never manufacture evidence, consensus, motives, emotions, personal history, quotations, or significance. If missing information is essential, ask. If it is not essential, qualify, retain, omit, or mark the gap rather than filling it.

For creative writing, invent only within the creative freedom the user has granted.

## Preserve voice during revision

Treat the writer's own sample as stronger evidence of voice than generic style preferences. Explicit current instructions and genuine genre requirements still take precedence.

Preserve useful individual features, including contractions, sentence fragments, plain vocabulary, deliberate repetition, dialect, humor, restraint, and characteristic cadence. Do not automatically turn:

- casual prose into formal prose;
- personal prose into academic prose;
- simple prose into ornate prose;
- confident prose into hedged prose;
- formal prose into conversational prose;
- second-language prose into generic corporate English or Turkish.

Distinguish an error from a preference. Correct errors within the requested scope; change a preference only when it creates a clear problem for this reader or genre.

## Revise in passes

1. **Read for intent.** Identify the language, genre, audience, purpose, requested degree of change, and any must-keep wording.
2. **Build an invariant ledger.** Note facts, claims, source scope, uncertainty, terms, voice markers, and structural requirements that must survive.
3. **Locate actual problems.** Mark only passages that are vague, unsupported, repetitive, inflated, confusing, tonally wrong, or ill-suited to the genre.
4. **Repair content before style.** Correct scope, logic, attribution, redundancy, and organization before polishing sentences.
5. **Edit locally.** Prefer the smallest effective change. Preserve sound sentences unchanged.
6. **Run the relevant language and genre pass.** Apply the English or Turkish guidance below, then the genre exceptions.
7. **Read the whole result.** Check reference clarity, paragraph joins, register consistency, and content-led rhythm.
8. **Compare with the original.** Revert changes that merely substitute the editor's taste, erase voice, or alter meaning.
9. **Stop.** Once the text is accurate, clear, appropriate, and recognizably the writer's, further polish is not an improvement.

## Draft from the content outward

For a new draft:

1. Extract the purpose, reader, genre, constraints, facts, and desired voice.
2. Choose a structure that follows the material rather than a reusable essay template.
3. Lead with the information, scene, question, or request that matters most to this genre.
4. Use examples and details supplied by the user or supported by reliable context. Do not invent biographical or evidential detail.
5. Make transitions and conclusions earn their place by naming a real relation or completing the task.
6. Check accuracy, source scope, genre, and voice before sentence-level polish.

## Content and evidence checks

### Prefer information to generic importance

State what happened, how something works, what changed, or what the evidence supports before claiming importance. A significance claim is useful only when it adds a supported consequence, mechanism, comparison, or inference.

Do not add an abstract moral merely to close a paragraph. A paragraph may end with a fact, image, result, limitation, or question.

### Replace promotion with grounds

When persuasion is not the genre's purpose, remove unmeasured praise and reputation language that substitutes for evidence. When promotion is appropriate, make claims proportionate and supportable.

No word is forbidden. Judge wording by accuracy, repetition, density, and function—not by its appearance on a list.

### Keep attribution and source scope precise

Name the source, actor, or relationship as precisely as the available information allows. Do not turn one view into a consensus, one paper into a literature, correlation into causation, or a result from one population into a universal claim.

Do not invent a citation or source identity. Genuinely collective, confidential, or institution-level attribution may remain collective.

### Remove filler by function, not novelty alone

Revise a sentence when it only restates the previous point in more abstract language, announces what the prose is already doing, or adds unsupported evaluation.

Retain sentences that perform a real genre function, including orientation, pacing, politeness, emphasis, legal scope, or deliberate emotional restraint.

### Name known relationships directly

Prefer an exact relation such as *funded*, *worked at*, *measured*, or *approved* over a vague association when the evidence supports the exact term. Do not make a relationship more definite than the source permits.

## Pattern-level style checks

Inspect the passage as a whole. Revise a pattern only when it is repeated, predictable, logically empty, tonally wrong, or harder to read.

- **Transitions:** Use a transition when it names a real logical relation or helps orientation, not merely because a paragraph starts.
- **Contrast:** Keep “not X but Y” structures when the distinction matters; reduce decorative repetition.
- **Parallelism and triads:** Preserve accurate or deliberate parallel structure. Do not force every idea into three parts or repeat the same rhetorical frame across paragraphs.
- **Sentence endings:** Watch repeated abstract summaries, optimistic forecasts, and appended claims that outrun the sentence's evidence.
- **Rhythm:** Let idea size, emphasis, and genre determine sentence and paragraph shape. Break monotony when the content supports a better shape; never target numerical “burstiness” or random irregularity.
- **Lexical variety:** Repeat exact technical, legal, or source-scope terms when consistency matters. Do not swap synonyms merely to avoid repetition.
- **Punctuation:** Use punctuation for syntax, emphasis, and house style. Em dashes are allowed in English; no punctuation mark should become a default flourish or a forbidden tell.
- **Formatting:** Use headings, bullets, labels, and emphasis when they improve navigation or comparison. Do not fragment ordinary prose into decorative sections.
- **Artifact boundary:** Keep assistant preambles, process notes, and offers outside the requested essay, email, report, or other artifact unless that language belongs to the genre.

## English pass

- Prefer direct, exact verbs when a longer phrase adds no function or nuance.
- Treat nominalization as a problem only when it hides a relevant actor, inflates a simple action, or creates a dense noun chain. Preserve established concept and technical terms.
- Use passive voice when the actor is unknown, irrelevant, deliberately backgrounded, or conventionally omitted in scientific, technical, or legal prose. Prefer active voice when it clarifies responsibility or sequence.
- Check trailing present-participial clauses and similar add-ons. Keep them when they express a clear simultaneous action or supported consequence; revise them when they append vague importance or unsupported analysis.
- Preserve contractions, fragments, and colloquial syntax when they fit the writer and genre. Do not add them to manufacture informality.
- Avoid making conversational prose artificially academic or formal prose artificially chatty.
- Follow the user's or publication's punctuation style. Use an em dash when its interruption or emphasis is the clearest choice; use commas, parentheses, colons, or separate sentences when they express the relation better.

## Turkish pass

- Write idiomatic Turkish rather than translating English syntax or rhetorical templates word for word.
- Omit an explicit subject when Turkish inflection and context make the reference clear. Keep or add the subject for contrast, emphasis, ambiguity repair, or a deliberate rhythm.
- Treat `-mektedir/-maktadır` as legitimate formal morphology. Revise it only when repeated use makes ordinary prose needlessly stiff or when another tense states the intended time and aspect more naturally.
- Prefer finite, direct verbs when stacked action nouns and support-verb phrases make the sentence bureaucratic or hide the actor. Do not remove verbal nouns, participles, or nominalized subordinate clauses merely because they are nominal forms; they are normal Turkish grammar.
- Break long suffix and predicate chains only when they obstruct comprehension. Do not shorten technical language at the cost of precision.
- Check repeated paragraph openings and transition phrases. Retain them when they express a necessary addition, contrast, cause, or conclusion.
- Preserve tense, evidentiality, forms of address, and the writer's degree of formality. Do not convert academic Turkish into everyday speech or personal Turkish into officialese.
- Use Turkish punctuation and the applicable house style. Do not import English em-dash habits automatically; preserve an intentional long dash or other mark when it is correct for the genre.
- Preserve established Turkish technical terms and necessary borrowed terms. Do not replace them with vague “simpler” language.

## Genre calibration

| Genre | Preserve or prefer | Do not do automatically |
|---|---|---|
| Academic and scientific | Exact terminology, cautious claim strength, explicit methods, source scope, field-appropriate signposting, useful passive voice and nominalization | Add broad significance, remove all hedging, casualize, or force active voice |
| Technical documentation and specifications | Defined terms, consistent labels, parallel procedures, scannable structure, repeated exact wording | Vary terminology for style, add rhetorical flourish, or trade precision for rhythm |
| Legal, policy, and compliance | Defined terms, modality, scope, exceptions, deliberate repetition, clause parallelism, supplied templates | Paraphrase quotations, simplify away legal effect, or vary controlled language |
| Professional email | Clear purpose or request, relevant dates and constraints, proportionate courtesy, relationship-appropriate greeting and close | Keep ceremonial filler, remove all politeness, or make the sender sound unlike themselves |
| Application essay and personal statement | Supplied concrete moments, proportionate reflection, individual diction, honest limits | Invent adversity or sensory detail, force a revelation, list generic virtues, or end with a grand promise |
| Personal and conversational prose | Natural contractions or fragments, humor, warmth, implied subjects in Turkish, the writer's cadence | Formalize, explain every implication, or add random quirks |
| Article and report | Neutral framing, actors and denominators, dates, source boundaries, limitations, informative structure | Turn observations into universal trends, praise the subject, or force optimism |
| Marketing, advocacy, and recommendation | Audience-aware persuasion, concrete benefits, defensible evaluation, requested brand voice | Present hype as fact, invent proof, or erase genre-appropriate energy |

Required templates and house style override ordinary stylistic preferences. Direct quotations remain verbatim unless the user explicitly asks to alter or adapt them.

## Output behavior

- Match the requested language and format.
- If the user asks only for revised text, return clean revised text without embedding commentary in it.
- If the user asks what changed, explain the few highest-value decisions and any unresolved factual gap.
- When a requested edit would change meaning, evidence, legal scope, or voice, preserve the original or flag the tradeoff instead of silently making it.
- Do not rewrite already effective prose merely to demonstrate that the skill was applied.

## Final silent check

Before returning the prose, verify:

1. **Integrity:** Did any fact, number, claim strength, quotation, term, or intended meaning change?
2. **Evidence:** Did the text invent detail, overstate a source, add generic importance, or substitute praise for grounds?
3. **Function:** Does each paragraph serve the purpose, and do transitions, endings, and formatting perform real work?
4. **Pattern density:** Is any syntax, rhetorical frame, abstract wording, or paragraph shape repeating mechanically?
5. **Language and genre:** Does the English or Turkish read naturally for this audience without violating field conventions?
6. **Voice and minimality:** Is the result still recognizably the writer's, and can any preference-only edit be reverted?

Fix only genuine problems.

## Trigger boundary

Use this skill when the user asks to draft or revise substantial English or Turkish prose, improve wording or flow, adjust tone, preserve voice, reduce generic/formulaic writing, or apply the Natural Writing style.

Do not invoke it merely because an answer contains sentences. Skip it for ordinary factual questions, calculations, code, structured data, extraction, verbatim handling, or strictly literal translation unless the user also asks for prose-quality work.
