# Natural Writing changelog

## v2

### Added

- Separate **Draft**, **Revise**, **Rewrite**, and **Strict handling** modes.
- Minimal-edit behavior as the default for user-authored text, with an explicit stop rule.
- An invariant ledger for facts, numbers, citations, claim strength, terminology, legal/technical scope, and voice.
- An explicit no-invention branch: ask, qualify, retain, omit, or flag missing detail rather than fabricating specificity.
- Separate English and Turkish passes, including Turkish subject omission, formal suffixes, grammatical nominalization, translationese, evidentiality, and punctuation conventions.
- Genre calibration for academic/scientific, technical, legal/policy, email, application, personal/conversational, report/article, and promotional prose.
- Negative-trigger rules so the skill does not activate for ordinary factual answers, code, extraction, verbatim text, or strictly literal translation.
- A compact final check for integrity, evidence, function, pattern density, genre/language, voice, and minimality.

### Changed

- Replaced the flat priority list with protected invariants and context-sensitive preferences.
- Reframed “AI-style” features as reader-facing problems of function, density, repetition, evidence, and genre fit.
- Changed the specificity test from “make specific or remove” to a constrained choice that never authorizes invented detail.
- Changed the necessity test so politeness, pacing, orientation, legal scope, and deliberate emphasis can count as useful work.
- Qualified advice about passives, nominalization, transitions, conclusions, triads, symmetry, contractions, formatting, and em dashes.
- Replaced “vary rhythm” with content-led sentence and paragraph shape; no numeric variation target.
- Replaced generic “light touch” exceptions with explicit genre requirements.
- Consolidated duplicated rules and the long final checklist into a shorter operational workflow.

### Removed

- Fixed English and Turkish inventories of allegedly model-like or promotional words.
- Any implication that one word, one em dash, one triad, one transition, polished grammar, or formal language is evidence of authorship.
- “Suspicious symmetry” and other detector-adjacent framing.
- Mechanical synonym variation and any rationale for planted errors, typos, fragments, or randomness.
- Automatic simplification that could erase technical terminology, defined terms, scientific passive voice, or legitimate Turkish morphology.

### Tested

- Five English and five Turkish positive cases across application, scientific, email, informal, and report prose.
- Four negative cases that must remain unchanged, including scientific methods and naturally irregular personal prose.
- Specific overcorrection risks: fabricated detail, stripped courtesy, damaged passive voice, lost terminology, casualized Turkish academic prose, pronoun-heavy translationese, and unnecessary lexical variation.

### Still provisional

- Broader judgments about “Turkish rhythm” beyond observable syntax and register.
- Genre-specific density thresholds for signposting, nominalization, and repeated formal suffixes.
- Performance on long documents and on highly individual bilingual voices.
- Reader preference and voice-preservation results under blinded, independent bilingual evaluation.
