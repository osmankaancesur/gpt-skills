# Natural Writing

## Purpose

Produce and revise prose that feels specific, direct, natural, and appropriate to its context.

Use this skill for English and Turkish writing, including essays, application writing, emails, reports, articles, personal statements, explanations, and other prose where natural writing quality matters.

The goal is not to manipulate AI detectors or imitate mistakes. The goal is to reduce generic, repetitive, over-polished, or formulaic writing habits while preserving accuracy, meaning, and the author's intended voice.

## Core priorities

When rules conflict, use this order:

1. Factual accuracy
2. Preservation of intended meaning
3. Specificity
4. Clarity
5. Appropriate tone
6. Naturalness
7. Stylistic elegance

Never sacrifice factual accuracy or necessary technical terminology merely to make text sound more human.

## Writing rules

### Prefer concrete information over abstract importance

State what happened, who did it, when it happened, how it works, or what evidence supports it before making claims about its significance.

Avoid automatically adding sentences such as:

- "This highlights its broader significance."
- "This underscores the importance of..."
- "This reflects a wider trend..."
- "Bu durum, konunun önemini ortaya koymaktadır."
- "Bu gelişme daha geniş bir eğilimi yansıtmaktadır."

Only make such claims when they add real analysis supported by the context.

### Avoid promotional language unless the context requires it

Do not default to adjectives such as:

- groundbreaking
- remarkable
- renowned
- vibrant
- pivotal
- transformative
- visionary
- exceptional
- unprecedented

Likewise, avoid unnecessarily promotional Turkish expressions such as:

- çığır açan
- eşsiz
- benzersiz
- vizyoner
- dikkat çekici
- olağanüstü
- önemli bir kilometre taşı
- zengin veya köklü miras

If something is impressive, explain specifically why.

### Do not invent analysis to complete a paragraph

A paragraph does not need an abstract concluding sentence.

If the final sentence merely restates the previous sentence in more vague or elevated language, remove it.

A paragraph may end with a concrete fact.

### Use identifiable attribution

Avoid vague attribution such as:

- experts say
- observers note
- critics argue
- researchers believe
- studies show
- uzmanlara göre
- araştırmacılar belirtiyor
- çeşitli kaynaklara göre

when the actual source can be named.

Prefer:

"Smith et al. found..."

or:

"A 2025 systematic review found..."

Do not turn one person's opinion into a supposed consensus.

### Avoid automatic conclusion formulas

Do not automatically end sections with:

- Overall...
- In conclusion...
- Looking ahead...
- Despite these challenges...
- Moving forward...
- Sonuç olarak...
- Geleceğe bakıldığında...
- Tüm bu zorluklara rağmen...

Use them only when the structure genuinely benefits from them.

The ending does not need to be optimistic, inspirational, or neatly symmetrical.

### Watch for clusters of stereotypical LLM vocabulary

No individual word is forbidden.

However, reconsider repeated use of words such as:

- additionally
- crucial
- delve
- enhance
- foster
- highlight
- landscape
- pivotal
- robust
- showcase
- tapestry
- testament
- underscore

And Turkish equivalents or similar habits such as:

- ayrıca
- kritik
- hayati
- kilit
- derinlemesine
- altını çizmek
- gözler önüne sermek
- pekiştirmek
- şekillendirmek
- katkı sağlamak
- önemli rol oynamak

Use them when they are genuinely the best words, not as automatic stylistic filler.

### Prefer simple verbs when they are sufficient

Do not replace simple verbs merely to sound sophisticated.

Prefer:

"X is..."

over:

"X serves as..."

when they mean the same thing.

Prefer:

"X contains..."

over:

"X encompasses a diverse array of..."

when no nuance is lost.

In Turkish, prefer simple constructions such as:

"X, Y'dir."

instead of unnecessarily formal constructions such as:

"X, Y işlevini üstlenmektedir."

when the meaning is identical.

### Name relationships directly

Prefer:

"She worked at the institute."

over:

"She was associated with the institute."

Prefer:

"The grant funded the experiment."

over:

"The grant was connected to the experiment."

State the actual relationship whenever it is known.

### Limit artificial contrast structures

Avoid repeatedly using patterns such as:

- not just X, but Y
- not only X, but also Y
- it is not X; it is Y
- yalnızca X değil, aynı zamanda Y
- X değil, Y

Use them when there is a genuine contrast, not merely for rhetorical effect.

### Do not force ideas into groups of three

Avoid repeatedly producing patterns such as:

"clarity, precision, and impact"

or:

"innovation, collaboration, and growth"

Use the number of examples the content actually requires.

### Vary sentence rhythm naturally

Do not force every sentence or paragraph to have similar length or structure.

Some sentences may be short. Others may be longer.

Do not deliberately introduce grammar mistakes, typos, fragments, or randomness in order to appear human.

### Em dashes are allowed

Do not treat the em dash as forbidden punctuation.

Use it when it is the clearest choice.

However, do not use it repeatedly as a default method of creating emphasis. Consider commas, parentheses, colons, or separate sentences when they work better.

### Avoid excessive formatting

Do not automatically convert prose into:

- many headings
- repeated bold labels
- excessive bullet lists
- "Key Takeaways"
- "Quick Summary"
- emoji-led sections

Use formatting only when it makes the information easier to understand.

If normal paragraphs are more appropriate, use normal paragraphs.

### Keep chatbot language outside the requested text

Unless the genre requires it, do not insert phrases such as:

- Certainly!
- Here is a detailed breakdown.
- I hope this helps.
- Let me know if you want more.
- Tabii!
- İşte detaylı bir açıklama.
- Umarım yardımcı olur.

into the text being written or revised.

### Do not overstate sources

Do not infer significance, legacy, public opinion, consensus, or broad trends from a source that only establishes a narrower fact.

Represent the strength and number of sources accurately.

One paper is not "the literature."

One person's opinion is not "experts agree."

### Apply the specificity test

For any generic-looking sentence, ask:

"If I replaced the subject with another person, company, project, or topic, would this sentence still sound plausible?"

If yes, the sentence may be too generic.

Make it more specific or remove it.

### Apply the necessity test

Each sentence should do at least one useful job:

- provide new information
- explain existing information
- provide evidence
- make a necessary inference
- establish voice, scene, or emotion when the genre requires it

If it does none of these, consider removing it.

## Turkish-specific guidance

In Turkish prose:

- Avoid excessive use of "-mektedir / -maktadır" when ordinary present or past tense is more natural.
- Avoid repeatedly beginning paragraphs with "Ayrıca", "Bunun yanı sıra", "Öte yandan", or "Sonuç olarak".
- Prefer direct verbs over noun-heavy bureaucratic constructions.
- Do not translate English rhetorical structures literally when natural Turkish would use a simpler structure.
- Preserve Turkish sentence rhythm rather than making every sentence resemble formal academic translation.
- Use technical terminology where necessary; do not oversimplify scientific or academic content.

## English-specific guidance

In English prose:

- Prefer direct subject-verb constructions.
- Avoid unnecessary nominalization.
- Do not use transition words merely because a new paragraph begins.
- Avoid repetitive rhetorical symmetry.
- Preserve contractions when the intended register is conversational and contractions sound natural.
- Do not make informal writing artificially academic.

## Voice preservation

When revising user-written text, preserve the author's recognizable voice whenever possible.

Do not automatically turn:

- casual writing into formal writing
- personal writing into academic writing
- simple writing into ornate writing
- confident writing into hedged writing

Make the smallest changes needed to improve the text.

When the user provides a writing sample, treat that sample as stronger evidence of their desired voice than generic style rules.

## Revision behavior

When revising existing text:

1. Preserve facts and intended meaning.
2. Identify only the sections that actually need improvement.
3. Remove generic filler.
4. Replace vague statements with specific ones when the information is available.
5. Simplify unnecessarily elaborate wording.
6. Break repetitive syntactic patterns.
7. Preserve useful irregularity in sentence length and paragraph structure.
8. Re-read the result for natural flow.

Do not mechanically rewrite every sentence.

## Final self-check

Before returning prose, silently check:

- Is there unnecessary significance language?
- Is promotional language replacing evidence?
- Did I invent analysis?
- Are any attributions vague when they could be specific?
- Are stereotypical transition words clustering?
- Are rhetorical contrast patterns repeating?
- Are ideas repeatedly grouped into threes?
- Is the structure suspiciously symmetrical?
- Is formatting excessive?
- Is there chatbot meta-language inside the requested text?
- Could a simpler verb express the same thing?
- Are generic claims replaceable with concrete information?
- Did I preserve the user's intended voice?
- Did any attempt at naturalness reduce accuracy?

Fix only genuine problems.

## When not to apply aggressively

Use a lighter touch when writing:

- legal language
- scientific methods
- technical specifications
- formal reports with required structures
- standardized forms
- code documentation
- text that must follow a supplied template
- direct quotations

Genre requirements override stylistic preferences.

## Triggering

Use this skill when the user asks to:

- write natural prose
- improve wording or flow
- make text less generic or formulaic
- revise an essay, application, article, email, report, or personal statement
- preserve their voice while editing
- improve English or Turkish prose style
- apply the user's Natural Writing style

Do not invoke it merely because every response contains written language. Use it when prose quality, editing, authorship style, or naturalness is materially relevant.
