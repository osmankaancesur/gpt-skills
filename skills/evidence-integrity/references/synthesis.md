# Traceable synthesis

Use when combining multiple sources into an answer whose claim-level support matters.

Group sources by the question and result they address, not by the order in which they were retrieved. Keep study design, population, outcome, time window and source dependence attached to findings until compatibility is established. A review, press release and original article about one study remain one evidential lineage.

Write the common ground, meaningful differences, and unresolved issues. Sources can agree at one level and disagree at another. An apparent conflict may reflect different measures or conditions; explain that possibility only when the sources support it. Otherwise preserve the disagreement.

Place citations so their support is unambiguous. Split a sentence when its clauses have materially different support. A citation at the end of a paragraph must not appear to validate unsupported earlier assertions. Trace secondary citations to originals when needed and available; otherwise attribute the claim to the source actually inspected.

Mark your synthesis or extrapolation as such where readers could mistake it for a reported result. Do not turn several interpretations into a consensus or aggregate incomparable numbers. Keep meaningful null and contrary findings even when they complicate the narrative.

Finish with what the combined material permits the reader to conclude, the limits that matter, and any requested implications. Keep it readable: a working evidence table is a verification aid, not a compulsory substitute for prose.
