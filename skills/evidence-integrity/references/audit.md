# Claim-support audit

Use for a supplied claim, report, or empirical argument whose evidential support is being reviewed.

Start with the conclusions that carry the argument or recommendation. Trace their necessary premises, then inspect the passages or results that supposedly support them. Include secondary claims when they change that assessment; do not give trivial wording issues equal weight.

Try to identify a decisive failure of the inference, not a quota of criticisms. Useful probes include a different explanation compatible with the data, a missing comparison, a subgroup standing in for the whole population, an endpoint changed between results and conclusion, or uncertainty omitted in the abstract. Name a probe as hypothetical until supported. Methodological criticism must connect a concrete feature of the work to a consequence for its inference.

For each material finding, give the claim/location, the supporting or conflicting evidence, the inference problem, and its consequence. Use an appropriate status:

- **Supported within scope:** the inspected evidence warrants the stated claim under its conditions.
- **Overstated:** a narrower claim is supported; identify the boundary crossed.
- **Unsupported in reviewed material:** necessary support is absent from what was inspected.
- **Contradicted:** inspected evidence conflicts with the claim; explain the conflict and scope.
- **Unresolved:** access or a specified specialist check prevents a judgment.

These labels are not a mandatory report template. Separate severity from confidence. A possible fatal problem is not an established fatal flaw. Classify a problem as conclusion-changing only when the conclusion depends on the failing step; separate repairable limitations from results that cannot currently stand.

When warranted, propose the narrowest defensible wording, missing analysis, or evidence needed. Do not silently replace the author's claim or invent a study that would rescue it. Preserve valid findings. If no material issue is found, say so within the reviewed scope rather than manufacturing objections.

Example: a single-arm pilot reports improvement among 12 completers. “The intervention caused improvement in all participants” adds causality and population coverage. The defensible statement reports the observed completer result; the review must also check enrollment, attrition and outcome definition before offering exact wording.
