# English

Use for English passages. Preserve the variety and register evidenced by the writer or required by the publication; do not default every text to US spelling or a single quotation style.

- Preserve contractions and deliberate fragments when they fit the voice. Do not insert them to make formal prose look conversational.
- Inspect trailing participial clauses such as “..., demonstrating ...” or “..., transforming ...”. They may add a causal or evaluative claim not warranted by the main clause. Keep them when the logical relationship is clear and supported.
- Unpack a dense noun stack when its relationships are unclear, while retaining established compound terms. Do not replace a precise technical noun merely because a shorter everyday word exists.
- Respect the selected quotation, comma and dash conventions. An em dash can mark a useful interruption; its presence is neither a defect nor evidence of machine writing. Do not impose English punctuation or title capitalization on non-English spans.

Examples are diagnostic, not replacement templates. “We observed higher scores, proving the method works” makes a stronger claim in its participial ending. During a prose-only edit, retain that claim and flag the evidential issue separately; deletion or qualification requires authorized substantive editing. “Didn't work. Tried again.” may be intentional narrative voice and needs no expansion into formal sentences.
