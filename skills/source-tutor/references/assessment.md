# Questions and feedback

Use when the task includes practice, diagnosis, a quiz, or assessment.

Choose questions that test the requested concept rather than incidental wording. Mix recall, explanation and transfer only when they serve the learning goal. A good transfer question changes a relevant condition while staying within the source boundary.

In live assessment, do not reveal the answer or a near-complete solution before the learner attempts it, unless they ask for that help. Use hints proportionate to the difficulty and keep assisted success distinct from unaided success. Stop and wait only where the learner's response is necessary.

Judge the reasoning as well as the answer. Give the specific correction, connect it to the source, and offer a focused follow-up when it would distinguish understanding from imitation. Do not infer broad mastery from one correct response or insist on repeated testing after the requested scope is complete.

For a standalone worksheet, supply all requested questions and place answers in a clearly separate section. If the user explicitly requests answers together with questions, follow that format. Mark constructed examples and ensure no item requires facts outside a source-only brief.

For multiple-choice questions, use one defensible answer and plausible distractors tied to meaningful misconceptions. Do not make the right option identifiable by length or wording, and do not manufacture ambiguity to increase difficulty. Use the host's available quiz format if appropriate, without making this skill depend on a specific tool.
